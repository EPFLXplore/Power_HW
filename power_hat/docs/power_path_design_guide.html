1The MOSFET as a controlled valve

Before sizing anything, you have to be clear about which region of the transistor the application drives it into. That single choice — fully-on switch vs. linear regulator — is what separates a gate driver / DC-DC FET from a hot-swap / eFuse pass FET, and it is the reason SOA matters here but not there.

An N-channel MOSFET conducts when the gate-to-source voltage VGS exceeds the threshold VGS(th). The channel it forms behaves in three regimes:

RegionConditionBehaviourWhere you see it
Cut-offVGS < VGS(th) No channel. ID≈0, the FET blocks VDS.Switch OFF
Ohmic / triodeVGS ≫ Vth, small VDS Acts like a resistor RDS(on). Loss = I²·RDS(on).Switch fully ON
Saturation / activeVGS > Vth, large VDS FET is a current source set by VGS: ID≈ ½·k·(VGS−Vth)². Dissipates ID·VDS — a lot.Inrush, current limit, linear reg.
Terminology trap — "saturation" does NOT mean "fully on" for a MOSFET. This is the opposite of a BJT and it confuses everyone. For a bipolar transistor, "saturation" = hard on, collector-emitter fully closed. For a MOSFET, "saturation" = the constant-current / active region: the channel is pinched off at the drain end, so ID stops rising with VDS and the device behaves as a current source — it is only partially on and dropping a large VDS. The MOSFET's "fully on" state is the ohmic / triode region (sometimes called the "linear" region because ID∝VDS like a resistor). So:
  • Switch fully ON → ohmic/triode (low VDS, acts as RDS(on)).
  • Hot-swap holding current → saturation/active (high VDS, current-source behaviour, lots of heat).
When this guide says the controller "parks the FET in saturation," it means exactly this partially-on, high-dissipation state — which is why SOA matters. (Annoyingly, datasheets also call this linear-mode operation "linear," meaning the third use of that word — the device acting as a linear regulator. Don't conflate it with the ohmic "linear region.")

Gate drivers & DC-DC converters: you live in the corners

In a buck/boost or a gate-driver-fed switch, the FET is slammed between cut-off and ohmic as fast as possible. The gate driver's whole job is to shove charge into the gate quickly so the device crosses the high-dissipation saturation region in nanoseconds. Loss is dominated by conduction (I²·RDS(on)) and by switching energy (the brief transit through saturation, ×fsw). You pick the FET on RDS(on), on gate charge Qg (drive loss & speed), and on Qgd (the Miller charge that sets dV/dt and shoot-through risk). SOA is almost irrelevant because the device never dwells in saturation.

Hot-swap / eFuse / current limiter: you deliberately sit in saturation

A hot-swap or current-limiting controller does the opposite. To tame inrush into a big output capacitor — or to hold the load current at a ceiling during a fault — it parks the FET in saturation on purpose, modulating VGS so the device behaves as a controlled current source. During that interval the FET simultaneously carries the full current and drops most of the bus voltage:

PFET = ID · VDS   with both large at once  →  this is the stress that the Safe Operating Area describes.linear-mode dissipation

This is why, for the TPS/ADM class of parts, the dominant transistor question is not "how low is RDS(on)" but "can the FET survive sitting in the linear region long enough for the controller's timer to act?"

The controller never touches the load directly. It only sets the gate voltage. Every amp that flows, and every volt dropped across the pass element, is a consequence of where the controller has positioned VGS on the transistor's I–V surface. Sizing the FET = making sure that surface, and its SOA, contains every operating point the controller will command.

2Two architectures on this board

The two protection parts solve the same problem with opposite topologies. Knowing which one hides the FET and which one exposes it tells you where you get to make transistor choices.

TPS25983 — integrated eFuse

The pass FET is inside the IC (RON ≈ 2.7 mΩ, 20 A). You do not size the main transistor — TI already guaranteed its SOA, and inrush is handled by an internal, dV/dt-controlled charge pump driving that internal gate. Your job is the passives that program the IC: current limit, slew rate, fault timer, UV/OV, current monitor.

The only external transistor in this path is an optional N-channel reverse-blocking FET driven by the dVdt/BGATE pin (here the CSD18540Q5B). It conducts forward through its body/channel and is turned off to block reverse current when the eFuse is disabled.

ADM1270 — external-FET current limiter

The controller has no internal pass device. It drives an external P-channel FET (here the SQJ461EP) through the GATE pin and senses current across an external RSENSE. A second P-FET on RPFG does reverse-polarity protection.

Because the FET is external, you own its SOA. The controller's TIMER + FLB (foldback) pins exist specifically to keep that FET inside its SOA during inrush and faults. This is the part that makes the gate-pin↔gate relationship visible, so we use it to teach §6.

Why this guide focuses on the TPS but borrows the ADM: the TPS25983 gives you a turnkey, guaranteed pass element and a rich set of passives to dimension — that's the bulk of the work on this board. But it hides the gate-control physics. The ADM1270 exposes exactly that physics (a literal GATE pin charging a literal MOSFET gate), so it is the perfect vehicle to explain inrush control, foldback, and FET/controller compatibility — knowledge you then apply when choosing the TPS's external BGATE FET.

3Transistor selection: SOA, the I–V plane, and charge

3.1 The output (I–V) characteristic — where the operating point lives

The output characteristic plots drain current vs. drain-source voltage for a family of gate voltages. It is the map of every state the controller can command. A switch lives hard against the left wall (ohmic); a hot-swap FET is dragged out into the flat plateaus (saturation) during start-up and faults.

3.2 The Safe Operating Area (SOA)

The SOA is the manufacturer's promise, on a log-log ID–VDS plot, of which (voltage, current) points the die survives for a given pulse duration before a hot-spot runs away (thermal instability / "spirito" effect). It is bounded by four limits:

  • RDS(on) line (top-left, slope +1): you can't have high current at low voltage beyond the on-resistance.
  • Current limit (top, horizontal): bond-wire / package max ID.
  • Power / thermal limits (the diagonal −1 slope lines): one per pulse width — 100 µs, 1 ms, 10 ms, DC. Shorter pulses allow more power.
  • VDS(max) line (right, vertical): avalanche breakdown.
SOA sign-off is a time question, not just a power question. "10 A at 24 V" might be inside the 1 ms line but far outside the DC line. So you cannot sign off a FET until you know how long the controller parks it there — which is set by the slew-rate cap (TPS CdVdt) or the timer cap (ADM CTIMER). Transistor sizing and passive sizing are the same calculation.

3.3 Inrush energy and the charge that must be delivered

During start-up the pass FET charges the entire downstream capacitance COUT from 0 to VBUS. Two "charge" budgets matter, and people mix them up:

(a) Load charge / inrush energy — sizes the FET's SOA

The charge moved into the load cap is Qload = COUT·VBUS. With a controlled, constant ramp the inrush current is flat:

Iinrush = COUT · dVdt = COUT · SR     tinrush = VBUSSR constant-dV/dt start-up

The energy the FET dissipates during that ramp (current flat, VDS falling linearly from VBUS to ~0) is:

EFET ≈ ½ · COUT · VBUS2 heat dumped into the die per start-up

This E and its duration tinrush are exactly the point you plot on the SOA. Slower ramp = lower Iinrush but longer dwell — you trade one SOA axis for the other, so you check both.

(b) Gate charge Qg — sizes the controller's drive capability & the ramp speed

This is the charge the controller must push into the gate to turn the FET on. It's read off the gate-charge curve. The flat Miller plateau (Qgd) is where VDS swings — the controller dwells there while the output ramps, so a weak gate pull-up current stretches the ramp:

4.1 The gate-clamp / VGS(max) interaction

Controllers protect the FET from over-drive with an internal gate clamp. The ADM1270 clamps ΔVGATE = VCC − VGATE to ~10–14 V (typ 12 V). So whatever the bus voltage, the P-FET sees at most ~12 V of |VGS|. That means:

  • The FET must be fully enhanced at ≤ that clamp — its RDS(on) spec at VGS=−10 V is the relevant one (SQJ461EP: 16 mΩ @ −10 V ✓).
  • The FET's VGS(max) must exceed the clamp (SQJ461EP: ±20 V > 12 V ✓), or the gate oxide is at risk.

The TPS25983's internal charge pump drives its internal gate to VIN + 3.6 V (that level is also what asserts PGOOD). For the external BGATE reverse FET, TI's BGATE driver and the chosen N-FET's VGS(max) must likewise be compatible — the CSD18540Q5B's ±20 V rating covers it.

Worked compatibility — Q5 = SQJ461EP on the ADM1270, ≈24 V bus, 10 A limit:
  • V: VDS,max 60 V > 24 V×1.3 ≈ 31 V ✓
  • Gate: enhanced at −10 V (RDS(on) = 16 mΩ); ADM clamp ~12 V < 20 V VGS(max) ✓
  • Charge: Qg ≈ 90 nC (140 max); IGATEDN ≈ 20 µA ⇒ gate ramp ≈ Qgd/20 µA → tens of ms, naturally soft-starting ✓
  • SOA: the check to actually plot — (≈24 V, 10 A) held for the 220 ms TIMER dwell (§6.4). This only passes because the foldback (§6.3) lowers the current while VDS is high; verify it on the SQJ461EP SOA curve.

5.1 RILIM — current-limit / circuit-breaker threshold

One resistor from ILIM to GND sets the overcurrent threshold (do not float, do not short — both are faults).

RILIM (Ω) = 1460ILIM(A) − 0.11  →  10 A → 147.6 Ω  ·  15 A → 98.0 Ω  ·  20 A → 73.4 Ω datasheet Eq. 5
Board status: Rillim1 is a placeholder (no value set). The IMON full-scale (§5.5) implies a design point near 10 A, which gives RILIM ≈ 147 Ω (E96, 1%). Pick the target current first, then commit this resistor. Do not float (threshold→0, instant trip) or short (fault).

Accuracy is ±8% for ILIM>5 A. The fast-trip (short-circuit) threshold IFT is internally fixed and reacts in <400 ns regardless of this resistor.

5.2 CdVdt — output slew rate & inrush control

This is the inrush knob. Pick the inrush current you'll allow, derive the slew rate, then the cap:

SR (V/ms) = Iinrush(mA)COUT(µF)     CdVdt(pF) = 4600SR(V/ms) Eq. 3 & 4

For reference, a pure-slew design limiting inrush to 3 A into 1000 µF ⇒ SR = 3000/1000 = 3 V/ms ⇒ CdVdt = 4600/3 ≈ 1.5 nF, giving start-up ≈ VBUS/SR = 24/3 = 8 ms.

Board status — this pin is wired as BGATE, not as a slew cap. On this board pin 15 (dVdt/BGATE) goes through RG1 = 100 Ω to the gate of Q1 (CSD18540Q5B, N-ch), with CdVdt1 = 0.1 µF on the net — i.e. the pin is being used in its BGATE role to drive the external reverse-current-blocking FET (§5.7), not purely to program output slew.
  • Taken literally as a slew cap, 0.1 µF (100 000 pF) gives SR = 4600/100000 ≈ 0.046 V/ms → a ~520 ms ramp at 24 V. That is almost certainly not the intended start-up time, which is the tell that this is BGATE-mode wiring rather than a dV/dt value.
  • Action: confirm the intended mode. The dVdt/BGATE pin serves one role at a time — if you need both a slow programmed slew and an external reverse FET, verify against TI's BGATE application circuit that the 100 Ω + 0.1 µF network matches their recommended gate components, and don't expect Eq. 3/4 to predict the ramp.
When the pin is used as a slew cap, CdVdt is what keeps the internal FET inside its SOA — it sets both the inrush current and the dwell time from §3.3, the integrated equivalent of the ADM's foldback + timer.

5.3 CITIMER — transient-fault blanking timer

Sets how long an overcurrent above ILIM (but below fast-trip) is tolerated before the breaker trips / current-limit engages — lets real load transients pass without nuisance trips.

tITIMER(ms) = CITIMER(nF) · ΔVITIMERIITIMER(µA) ,   ΔVITIMER≈0.98 V, IITIMER≈2.1 µA Eq. 6

Board: Citimer1 = 47 nF ⇒ tITIMER = 47 nF × 0.98 V / 2.1 µA ≈ 21.9 ms of overcurrent blanking before the breaker/limiter engages. Open = trip immediately; short = fault.

Upper bound (Eq. 7): too-large CITIMER hasn't charged past the pin-short detect level by the time start-up finishes → false fault. Keep CITIMER < 53000 · tGHI/(VIN+3.6), where tGHI = tD,ON + CdVdt·(VIN+3.6)/IdVdt. If they conflict, enlarge CdVdt (longer start-up) to buy timer room.

5.4 UVLO & OVLO dividers (EN/UVLO and OVLO pins)

Two resistor dividers from the bus. Same divider form, different reference:

VIN(UV) = VUVLO(F) · RVL1+RVL2RVL2     VIN(OV) = VOV(F) · RVL1+RVL2RVL2 Eq. 1 & 2

Pin thresholds are ~1.2 V class (VUVLO) / ~1.2 V (VOVLO) — read the exact rising/falling values from the EC table for your turn-on/off points and hysteresis. Size the divider so its current is ≥20× the pin leakage (datasheet rule) — e.g. total 1–2 MΩ keeps standby loss low while staying 20× above the µA-class pin bias. Tighter ratios (lower R) trade a little quiescent current for better accuracy.

Board status: the UVLO divider (R1, R2) and OVLO divider (R2, R3) are placeholders (symbol value "R_Small", no resistance set). R2 is the shared middle leg of the two dividers. These set the turn-on floor and the overvoltage cutoff — finalize them so OVLO trips below the 26 V max (e.g. ~26 V OV, with a UV floor matched to the minimum pack voltage). Until then the eFuse has no programmed UV/OV protection.

5.5 RIMON — analog current monitor

IMON sources a current proportional to load current; RIMON to GND converts it to a voltage for your ADC.

VIMON = ILOAD · RIMON · GIMON,   GIMON ≈ 243 µA/A Eq. 8

Board: Rimon1 = 1.33 kΩ ⇒ VIMON = ILOAD × 1330 Ω × 243 µA/A = 0.323 V/A. So the monitor reaches the 3.3 V ceiling at ≈10.2 A — i.e. the board is scaled for a ~10 A full-scale measurement, which is the basis for the 10 A current-limit recommendation in §5.1. The filter Rimon_filter = 10 kΩ + Cimon_filter = 0.1 µF gives fc ≈ 1/(2π·10 k·0.1 µF) ≈ 159 Hz into the ADC, and satisfies the ≥10 kΩ series recommendation. Keep VIMON ≤ 3.3 V for amplifier headroom (Table 7-2).

5.6 PGOOD, retry config, input & output capacitors

  • PGOOD (pin 13): open-drain, active-high. On this board it is left unconnected — no power-good feedback to the rest of the system. If you want it, add a pull-up (10–100 kΩ) to the logic rail; it asserts once the internal gate reaches VIN+3.6 V.
  • Retry behaviour: Cretry_dly1 = 220 pF on RETRY_DLY sets the delay between auto-retries, and R4 = 0 Ω on NRETRY selects the retry count / latch-vs-auto-retry behaviour — confirm the resulting mode against the datasheet's NRETRY table is what you want (auto-retry vs latch-off after a persistent fault).
  • Input caps: the board fits Cin2/Cin3 = 10 µF (T521D106, 50 V) and Cin1/Cin4 = 4.7 µF (T521D475, 63 V) low-ESR tantalum bulk on the EFUSE input rail, plus the SMCJ28A TVS (D1). Keep a local ceramic right at the IN pins as well to damp hot-plug di/dt and avoid nuisance fast-trips.
  • Output cap (COUT): on /Efuse_out — this is the load capacitance that sets inrush. If the dVdt/BGATE pin is in slew mode, bigger COUT ⇒ slower SR needed ⇒ larger CdVdt ⇒ longer start-up. Re-check §5.2 whenever output bulk changes.

5.7 The one external transistor: BGATE reverse-blocking N-FET — Q1 (CSD18540Q5B)

On this board Q1 = CSD18540Q5B (60 V N-ch) is driven from pin 15 through RG1 = 100 Ω, with CdVdt1 = 0.1 µF on the gate net. The TPS25983 drives this external N-FET (source toward the bus, body diode pointing forward) so that when the eFuse is OFF/disabled it actively blocks reverse current that would otherwise flow through a normal FET's body diode. Sizing it:

  • VDS(max) ≥ bus + margin → 60 V CSD18540Q5B on ≤26 V ✓ (very comfortable).
  • RDS(on) low — it's in the forward conduction path continuously, so it adds to total path loss on top of the 2.7 mΩ internal FET. The CSD18540Q5B's milliohm-class RDS(on) at the BGATE drive level keeps this negligible.
  • VGS(th) / VGS(max) compatible with the BGATE driver swing (CSD: ~1.9 V th, ±20 V max ✓).
  • It is not a linear pass element here (the internal FET does inrush), so its SOA stress is mild — the §4 checks 1–3 dominate, check 4 is light.

6.1 How the GATE pin controls current flow

The pass element is a high-side P-channel FET. A P-FET turns on as its gate is pulled below its source (the bus). The controller expresses this as ΔVGATE = VCC − VGATE: the bigger ΔVGATE, the harder the FET is on.

  • To turn ON: an internal current source IGATEDN (~20 µA) pulls the gate down, charging the gate capacitance. Because it's a tiny, controlled current charging Qg, VGATE ramps slowly → VGS ramps slowly → the FET creeps up its I–V curves → the output ramps gently and inrush is bounded. This is the same mechanism as the TPS's CdVdt, but here the "capacitor" is the FET's own gate and the "current" is the GATE pin source current.
  • To regulate / limit: the current-sense amp (gain 40) watches VSENSE=ILOAD·RSENSE. The moment it hits the reference, the loop stops driving the gate further and instead servos VGATE to hold the FET exactly on the saturation curve that produces ILIM. The transistor is now a regulator: VGS is whatever it must be so ID=ILIM.
  • To turn OFF / fault: a stronger IGATEUP (~50–80 µA, mA-class in fault) pulls the gate back up to VCC, removing VGS and opening the switch.
The relationship in one line: the controller pin doesn't "send current to the load" — it charges/discharges the FET gate, and the gate voltage positions the transistor's operating point on its I–V surface. Current limiting is the controller refusing to let VGS go any higher than the value that yields ILIM.

6.2 Setting the current limit — RSENSE and ISET

The sense amp trips at a programmable VSENSE from 12.5 mV to 62.5 mV (default 50 mV when ISET is tied to VCAP; adjust with a divider from VCAP into ISET).

ILIM = VSENSE(limit)RSENSE  →  board: RSENSE = R33 = 5 mΩ, ISET tied to VCAP (50 mV) ⇒ ILIM = 50 mV / 5 mΩ = 10 A matches the ~10 A scaling of the TPS path

The two paths are therefore both dimensioned around ~10 A. The sense resistor dissipates I²·RSENSE ≈ 10²·0.005 = 0.5 W at the limit — size R33's power rating and use a Kelvin (4-wire) connection to the SENSE pins. If you later want a different limit without changing R33, retune it with an ISET divider from VCAP (12.5–62.5 mV range).

6.3 Foldback (FLB) — the SOA insurance that the TPS does internally

Here is the "delicate" part. During inrush the FET sees the full bus across VDS while carrying the limit current — the worst SOA corner (§3.2). Foldback fixes it: a divider from the FET source (≈VOUT) to FLB reduces the current limit while VDS is large, then restores it as the output rises and VDS collapses. The reference selector always uses the lower of ISET and FLB.

current-limit ref = min(VISET, VFLB),   clamped so VFLB ≥ 200 mV (≡ 10 mV across RSENSE, never zero) keeps the start-up point under the SOA curve

Design the FLB divider so VFLB = VISET/2 occurs when VOUT has dropped to the level you want the foldback to kick in. This is precisely the job CdVdt does inside the TPS — only there it's a fixed dV/dt ramp rather than a VDS-dependent current reduction.

Board: the foldback divider is R34 = 90.9 kΩ (top, from the FET source) and R35 = 174 Ω (bottom, shared with the FB_PG divider whose top leg is R36 = 4 kΩ). Verify the resulting VFLB(VOUT) curve drives the start-up operating point (≈24 V across the FET at ILIM) under the SQJ461EP SOA, given the 220 ms TIMER dwell below — this is the single most important SOA check on the ADM path.

6.4 TIMER — bounding the SOA dwell time

While the FET sits in current limit (in saturation, dissipating heat) the TIMER cap charges from a ~20 µA source. When it reaches ~2 V the controller declares an SOA fault and turns the FET off:

tSOA = CTIMER · VTIMERHITIMERUP = CTIMER · 2.0 V20 µA  →  board: C40 = 2.2 µF ⇒ tSOA = 2.2 µF·2 V/20 µA = 220 ms the FET must survive its worst-case point for 220 ms

This closes the loop with §3.2: read the SQJ461EP's SOA at (≈24 V, the folded ILIM) and confirm the 220 ms dwell sits under the relevant SOA line — 220 ms is close to the DC SOA limit, so this is a demanding point and depends heavily on the foldback (§6.3) actually lowering the current while VDS is high. TIMER_OFF = C41 = 100 nF sets the cool-down before an auto-retry, giving the die time to recover (retry duty cycle).

Sanity-check the 220 ms dwell against the SOA. A 2.2 µF TIMER cap is a long time to hold a P-FET in linear mode at full bus voltage. If the foldback divider doesn't fold the current back hard enough at low VOUT, the SQJ461EP could leave its SOA before the timer expires. Either confirm the foldback curve covers it, or shorten C40.

  • Choose Iinrush, compute SR and CdVdt; record start-up time = VBUS/SR.
  • CITIMER for the blanking window; check it against the Eq. 7 upper bound.
  • UVLO / OVLO dividers: set thresholds, keep divider current ≥20× pin leakage, OVLO trips <26 V.
  • RIMON for full-scale ADC voltage; VIMON≤ table limit; add ≥10 kΩ RC filter.
  • PGOOD pull-up to correct logic rail; CIN local ceramic; COUT consistent with §5.2.
  • External FETs (BGATE N-FET, ADM P-FETs): run all four §4 checks — V, gate drive vs clamp, Qg/Igate ramp, SOA at the controlled point.
  • For the ADM path: RSENSE from ILIM; FLB divider for foldback; CTIMER shorter than the FET's SOA pulse width at (VBUS, ILIM).
Single most common mistake: signing off a pass FET on RDS(on) and steady-state current alone. In these parts the FET's survival is decided in the linear region during inrush/fault — always plot the (VDS, ID, t) point on the datasheet SOA and make the timer/slew passive enforce that t.
